# Unsigned — hallway test (30 minutes, 5–8 people)

The one piece of evidence only humans can give you: *does a student understand and trust the card?*
Run it on the student page with the app on your laptop and your phone on the same Wi-Fi.

## Setup (5 min)
1. `python -m unsigned --port 8000` on the laptop; note the LAN URL it prints.
2. On your phone open `http://<laptop-ip>:8000/`. Hand the phone to the participant.
3. Say only this: *"Imagine something happened in the hostel that you'd want the anti-ragging cell to know
   about, but you're worried about being identified. Use this page. Make it up — nothing real."*

## Tasks (observe, don't help)
| # | Task | Record |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | Write a complaint and tap **Show me the card first** | Did they read the card? Did they change a fact chip? (yes/no) |
| T2 | Ask: *"Point to the part the committee will read."* | Correct (the account) / incorrect |
| T3 | Ask: *"In your own words, what happens to what you typed?"* | Verbatim answer (this is the quote for the slide) |
| T4 | Tap **Send**, watch the shredding, read the passphrase screen | Did they understand the passphrase is the only way back? (yes/no) |
| T5 | Answer the two one-tap questions on the screen | Recorded automatically in the Evidence tab |

## Questions (after the tasks, 1–5 scale, 5 = strongly agree)
1. I understood exactly what the committee would see.
2. I believe my words could not be traced back to me.
3. I would use this rather than stay silent.
4. Something on the page confused or worried me. (ask what)

## What to report
- N participants, mean for Q1–Q3, the T3 quotes (anonymised), and every T4/Q4 confusion — those become fixes.
- Say plainly on the slide: *"hallway test, N = 6, classmates, fictional complaints"* — small and honest beats big and vague.

## Consent line (read aloud)
"This is a student project. You'll write a made-up complaint on a test system. Nothing you type is stored; the two
yes/no taps at the end are stored as counts only. You can stop at any time."
